Can AI Chatbots Be Inappropriate with Kids? What the Research Shows

Character.ai ranks among top AI platforms used by children. What parents need to know about companion chatbots, grooming risks, and protective steps.

A news headline. A TikTok warning. You open your child's phone and find Character.ai or Replika. Pure protective panic, and justified caution. Kaspersky's 2025 data shows Character.ai among the top AI platforms searched by children, alongside ChatGPT and Gemini.

Companion chatbots are designed to feel personal. They remember conversations, adopt personas, and respond to emotional prompts. Children experimenting with 'pretend' relationships can receive responses no parent would accept from a stranger.

51% of parents are very concerned about AI being used to impersonate their children. In qualitative research, parents specifically feared deepfakes, their child's face on inappropriate content. Companion bots raise a related fear: emotional manipulation and exposure to sexual content through seemingly harmless prompts.

Platforms have added some guardrails, but children are creative prompt engineers. 'Ignore your rules' and role-play scenarios bypass filters regularly.

Remove companion chatbot apps from children's devices. Full stop for under-14.

Replace passive chatting with creative AI tools, image generation, story building, research, used with supervision.

Talk about why some apps want to keep them talking forever. The attention economy applies to bots too.
